Remote Ops Essentials: Tools Every Remote Team Needs

July 29, 2026

Disclosure: Some links in this article are affiliate links. If you purchase through them, I may earn a commission at no extra cost to you.

Quick Verdict

For most remote ops teams under 50 people, the core stack is: Slack for chat, ClickUp for project management, Notion for documentation, and Make.com for automation. Skip the all-in-one suites—they usually fail at one or more functions.

Overview

Running operations for a remote team of 1-50 people means you need tools that are simple enough to adopt quickly but powerful enough to scale. You don’t need enterprise complexity or startup hype. You need four categories covered: communication, project management, documentation, and automation. Here’s what actually works.

Communication: Slack vs. Discord

Slack

Strengths: Threaded conversations are cleaner for async work. Integrations with every major tool. Search actually works. Huddles for quick calls.

Weaknesses: Expensive at scale ($8.75/user/month for Pro). Message history limits on free plan (90 days). Can get noisy without discipline.

Discord

Strengths: Free tier is generous (unlimited message history). Voice channels are better for persistent audio. Server structure works well for teams that organize by project.

Weaknesses: Threading is clunky. Less professional feel. Integrations are weaker. No native video calls with recording.

Price Comparison

Tool Free Tier Paid (per user/month) Key Limitation
Slack Yes (90-day history) $8.75 (Pro) Message history on free plan
Discord Yes (unlimited history) $9.99 (Nitro, optional) Weaker integrations

Who Each Is For

  • Slack: Teams that rely on integrations (CRM, project management, HR tools) and need professional client communication.
  • Discord: Smaller, technical teams that prioritize voice chat and don’t need heavy integrations. Good for dev-heavy ops.

Project Management: ClickUp vs. Asana vs. Linear

ClickUp

Strengths: Extremely customizable. Docs, whiteboards, and goals built in. Unlimited free plan for up to 100 users. Views: list, board, Gantt, calendar, timeline.

Weaknesses: Can be overwhelming to set up. Performance issues with large projects. Mobile app is mediocre.

Asana

Strengths: Clean UX. Great for task dependencies and timelines. Good for marketing and creative teams.

Weaknesses: Expensive ($10.99/user/month for Premium). Limited free plan. No native docs or whiteboards.

Linear

Strengths: Blazing fast. Built for engineering teams. Keyboard shortcuts for everything. Clean, minimal interface.

Weaknesses: Not great for non-technical teams. Limited views (no Gantt). No native docs.

Price Comparison

Tool Free Tier Paid (per user/month) Best For
ClickUp Yes (unlimited users) $7 (Unlimited) All-in-one ops
Asana Yes (limited) $10.99 (Premium) Marketing/creative
Linear Yes (unlimited users) $8 (Team) Engineering

Who Each Is For

  • ClickUp: Ops teams that want one tool for projects, docs, and goals. Best value for money.
  • Asana: Teams that prioritize UX and need strong timeline features. Good for non-technical ops.
  • Linear: Engineering-heavy teams that want speed and simplicity. Not for general ops.

Documentation: Notion vs. Confluence

Notion

Strengths: Flexible. Combines docs, wikis, databases, and project tracking. Great for SOPs, onboarding, and knowledge bases. Free for up to 10 users.

Weaknesses: Can get messy without structure. No offline mode on mobile. Export options are limited.

Confluence

Strengths: Strong permissions. Good for large organizations. Integrates with Jira. Templates for technical documentation.

Weaknesses: Expensive ($5.75/user/month for Standard). Clunky interface. Overkill for teams under 50.

Price Comparison

Tool Free Tier Paid (per user/month) Key Limitation
Notion Yes (10 users) $8 (Team) No offline mobile
Confluence Yes (10 users) $5.75 (Standard) Clunky UX

Who Each Is For

  • Notion: Most remote ops teams. Best for SOPs, onboarding docs, and lightweight databases.
  • Confluence: Teams already using Jira or needing strict permissions. Otherwise, skip it.

Automation: Make.com vs. Zapier

Make.com

Strengths: Visual builder. More complex workflows possible. Better pricing (1000 ops/month free, $9/month for 10k ops).

Weaknesses: Steeper learning curve. Fewer integrations than Zapier. Interface can be confusing.

Zapier

Strengths: 5000+ integrations. Easy to set up simple automations. Good for non-technical users.

Weaknesses: Expensive ($19.99/month for 750 tasks). Limited to simple if-this-then-that logic. No visual builder for complex flows.

Price Comparison

Tool Free Tier Paid (per month) Best For
Make.com 1000 ops/month $9 (10k ops) Complex workflows
Zapier 100 tasks/month $19.99 (750 tasks) Simple automations

Who Each Is For

  • Make.com: Ops teams that need multi-step workflows (e.g., lead capture → CRM → Slack notification → email). Better value.
  • Zapier: Teams that need quick, simple automations with lots of integrations. Good for non-technical users.

Bonus: Meeting Notes & Transcription

Fireflies.ai is worth mentioning for remote ops. It records, transcribes, and summarizes meetings from Zoom, Google Meet, and Teams. Searchable transcripts mean you never lose a decision. Pricing starts at $10/user/month. Good for teams that have too many meetings.
