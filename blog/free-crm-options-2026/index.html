Free CRM Options for Small Business in 2026

September 05, 2026

Quick Verdict

HubSpot’s free CRM still leads for pipeline management, but Zoho CRM Free is a strong contender if you can tolerate the UI. Both handle 1-50 person teams without forcing you into enterprise pricing.

Overview

Small ops teams need CRM that does three things well: track deals, log contacts, and automate follow-ups. The free tier should handle at least 1,000 contacts and 2-3 pipelines. Anything less becomes a glorified spreadsheet. Here are the real free options that won’t cap your growth at 10 users or 5 deals.

I tested five tools in January 2026. My criteria: free tier usable for a team of 5+, at least basic pipeline visualization, and no hidden per-user fees for essential features. Here’s what worked and what didn’t.

Head-to-Head: HubSpot vs. Zoho CRM Free vs. Freshsales

HubSpot Free CRM

Strengths: Unlimited users. 1,000,000 contacts (but only 1,000 stored). Pipeline management with deal stages. Email tracking and meeting scheduler built in. The most polished onboarding experience — your team will actually use it.

Weaknesses: Limited to 1 deal pipeline (you can have multiple, but they count toward your 1). No workflows or automation on free tier. Reporting is basic. You’ll get upgrade prompts constantly.

Zoho CRM Free

Strengths: 3 users free. Up to 5,000 contacts. Leads, deals, and accounts. Mass email integration with Zoho Mail. Blueprint for approval processes (limited). The free tier includes web-to-lead forms and custom fields.

Weaknesses: UI feels dated. Mobile app is slow. Email integration with Gmail/Outlook requires paid add-on. Workflow automation is limited to 5 rules. Support is nearly nonexistent on free plan.

Freshsales (Freshworks) Free

Strengths: Unlimited users. 10,000 contacts. Built-in phone and email (limited to 50 calls/user/month). Visual pipeline with drag-and-drop. AI-powered lead scoring is included — no extra charge.

Weaknesses: No workflow automation at all on free tier. Reporting is very basic. Cannot create custom modules. Mobile app is clunky for deal management.

Who Each Is For

HubSpot Free CRM

Best for teams that need a dead-simple pipeline that everyone will actually use. If you have 5-15 people who need to see deal stages and assign tasks, go HubSpot. The unlimited users is the killer feature — you won’t have to upgrade until you need real automation.

Skip if: You need automated email sequences, custom reporting, or more than one pipeline. The free tier is a deliberate gateway to their paid plans.

Zoho CRM Free

Best for data-heavy teams that need custom fields and modules. If you want to track custom deal types or have a sales process that requires approval flows, Zoho’s Blueprint feature on free is unique. The 3-user limit is fine for most 1-10 person ops teams.

Skip if: You hate clunky interfaces or need to integrate with Gmail without paying. The mobile experience will frustrate your field reps.

Freshsales Free

Best for teams with large contact lists (10,000 contacts free). The AI lead scoring is genuinely useful — it surfaces hot leads without you configuring anything. Unlimited users means your entire org can log calls and emails.

Skip if: You need workflow automation or sophisticated reporting. The free tier is basically a shared spreadsheet with better visuals.

Beyond the Basics: What You’ll Actually Need

Every free CRM lacks one thing: automation. You’ll eventually want to send follow-up emails, score leads, or notify Slack when a deal moves stage. For that, you’ll need either a paid CRM plan or a separate automation tool. I use Make.com to bridge these gaps — it connects free CRMs to email, Slack, and payment tools without per-user pricing. A typical setup costs $9/month for 1,000 operations.

If you’re using HubSpot Free + Make.com, you can build a lead capture → auto-reply → Slack notification flow in 30 minutes. The CRM handles the database; Make handles the logic.
