Free CRM Options for Small Business in 2026

September 21, 2026

Disclosure: Some links below are affiliate links. If you purchase through them, I may earn a commission at no extra cost to you. This helps keep the lab running. I only recommend tools I have tested or actively use.

Quick Verdict

For most small ops teams, HubSpot Free remains the best all-rounder for pipeline management, but it punishes you with data limits once you scale. If you need unlimited contacts at zero cost, Pipedrive (via its low-cost tier) or Zoho CRM are better bets. Do not pay for a CRM until you have more than 5,000 contacts or need advanced automation — free tiers cover 80% of pipeline needs.

Overview

Let’s be blunt: most "free CRM" lists are full of marketing fluff. For an ops lead at a 10-person company, the real question is not "which has the best UI" but "which won't force me to migrate in 12 months." In 2026, the free tier landscape has shifted. The big players now gate their best features (email sequences, reporting, API access) behind paid plans. But a few solid options remain for pure pipeline management.

This comparison focuses on tools that give you a visual sales pipeline, contact management, and basic email tracking without requiring a mortgage. I have excluded enterprise-only tools like Salesforce (obviously) and niche tools that are too limited for real ops work.

Head-to-Head: The 2026 Free Tier Reality

1. HubSpot CRM (Free)

Still the default, but with caveats. The free plan offers unlimited users and deals, which is rare. You get a clean pipeline view, contact timelines, and basic email tracking (2,000 emails/month). The weakness? Data silos. Once you hit 1 million lifetime contacts (yes, that is the limit), or if you want to automate workflows beyond simple sequences, you are pushed to the paid tiers starting at $20/user/month. For a 5-person team, that is $100/month just to unlock "enroll in sequence."

Verdict: Use it if you are under 500 contacts and want a safe, well-documented tool. Migrate away if you are a B2B company with high volume.

2. Zoho CRM (Free Tier)

Zoho's free plan is shockingly generous: 3 users, 5,000 records, and access to pipelines, custom fields, and email integration. It is not as pretty as HubSpot, but it is more powerful for the price (free). The interface feels dated, and the mobile app is clunky. However, for a 10-person ops team that needs to track leads without paying per seat, Zoho is hard to beat.

Verdict: Best for teams that hate spending money and need custom fields without paying a premium.

3. Pipedrive (14-day trial, then $14/user/month)

Pipedrive does not have a true free plan, but I am including it because its entry-level tier is often cheaper than HubSpot's paid upgrades. The pipeline is visual and drag-and-drop, which is superior to HubSpot's list-based view. The catch? You pay for the privilege of seeing your pipeline. The free trial exports your data cleanly, but you will not want to leave once you are in.

Verdict: Pay for it only if you have outgrown free CRMs and need a simple, fast pipeline without the bloat of enterprise tools.

4. ClickUp (Free Plan)

ClickUp is not a pure CRM, but its free plan includes a decent pipeline view and unlimited tasks. If your sales process is tied to project delivery (e.g., agency work), ClickUp might replace both your CRM and project management tool. However, using it purely for sales is awkward — the contact records are thin, and email tracking is limited.

Verdict: Choose this if you want to consolidate tools and your sales process is really just a project workflow. Do not choose it if you need robust lead scoring.

Who Each Is For

  • HubSpot Free: Your team is under 10 people, you send less than 2,000 emails a month, and you want a tool that grows with you (but you are okay paying later).
  • Zoho CRM: You have under 3 sales reps, need custom fields, and refuse to pay for basic CRM features. You do not mind a 2010-era UI.
  • Pipedrive: You have a clear sales process, need a simple visual pipeline, and are willing to pay a small monthly fee to avoid the limitations of free tools.
  • ClickUp: You are an ops team that also handles delivery. You want one tool for tasks and a light CRM, not a dedicated sales tool.

Quick Verdict (Revisited)

If I were setting up a 15-person ops team today, I would start with Zoho CRM for its free limits, and set a calendar reminder to migrate to Pipedrive once we hit 5,000 contacts. HubSpot is fine, but its free tier is a trap — you will outgrow it quickly, and the migration is painful. Do not pay for a CRM until you have a dedicated sales person who is complaining about the tool, not the process.
