Automate Email Triage with Make.com and AI

November 03, 2026

Disclosure: this post contains affiliate links. If you sign up through them, Shayne's AI Lab earns a commission at no extra cost to you. We only link to tools we've actually run.

Quick Verdict

Make.com plus a cheap LLM is the lowest-cost way to get a working email triage system for a team under 50 — roughly $10–20/month in Make operations and a few cents in AI tokens. It's the wrong choice if you need sub-second routing, want zero maintenance, or don't have anyone willing to own a visual workflow.

Overview: What "Email Triage Automation" Actually Means

Most small ops teams don't have a triage problem — they have a sorting problem. Someone reads every inbound message to decide whether it's a support ticket, a vendor pitch, a billing question, or a customer who needs a human in the next hour. That's 30–90 minutes a day of low-value work.

A Make.com scenario can do the sorting. The build is six steps:

  • Trigger — Gmail "Watch Emails" or Outlook "Watch Messages," filtered to unread inbox mail.
  • Pre-filter — a Make filter that drops known newsletter domains and internal senders before you spend an AI call on them. This alone cuts 30–50% of volume for most teams.
  • AI classification — the OpenAI module (or an HTTP call to Anthropic) with a prompt that returns strict JSON: {"category": "...", "priority": "high|normal|low", "needs_reply": true, "summary": "one line", "owner": "..."}. Use JSON mode so parsing doesn't break.
  • Parse JSON — a separate module, not a text parser. This is where most broken builds fail.
  • Router — branches by category. High-priority sales mail goes to Slack; support mail creates a ClickUp task in the support list; everything else gets a Gmail label and stays put.
  • Log — append a row to Google Sheets with sender, category, priority, and the AI's one-line summary. You'll want this for the first month to audit accuracy.

Realistic accuracy on a well-prompted model with 6–8 categories: 85–92%. That's good enough to sort, not good enough to auto-archive. Keep a human in the loop on anything destructive for at least the first 60 days.

Latency is 5–15 seconds per email, which is fine for triage and useless for anything real-time. Token cost is negligible: at GPT-4o-mini rates, a 500-token prompt plus a 60-token response runs about $0.0001 per email. A thousand emails a month costs roughly a dime.

Head-to-Head: Make vs. Zapier vs. n8n

All three can run this scenario. The differences show up in cost per email and how much you'll fight the tool.

Make.comZapiern8n
Ops per email ~4–5 ~4–5 tasks ~4–5 executions
Cost at 2,000 emails/mo ~$10–19 ~$30–50 $0 self-hosted / ~€20 cloud
Visual builder Strong — routers and iterators are first-class Simplest, but branching gets ugly fast Node graph, steeper learning curve
Error handling Built-in error routes and Break/Resume Basic retries, limited fallbacks Full control, you build it
Maintenance Low Lowest Highest — you own the server

Make wins on price-per-operation and on branching. A router with four paths is trivial; the same logic in Zapier becomes four separate Zaps that drift out of sync. The weakness is the operation model — every module execution counts, so a careless loop or a "list all labels" call can burn through quota fast.

Zapier is the right answer if nobody on your team wants to think about workflows. It's also 3–5x the cost at this volume, and the free tier (100 tasks) won't survive a week of real inbox traffic.

n8n is the pick if you have a developer who'll own it. Self-hosted is free and unlimited, and the AI nodes are more flexible than Make's. But "free" means someone patches the container and debugs the Postgres connection, and at a 10-person company that someone is usually the person who's already busiest.
