Automate Email Triage with Make.com and AI

September 09, 2026

Disclosure: Some links on this page are affiliate links. If you choose to purchase through them, I may earn a small commission at no extra cost to you.

Quick Verdict

You can build a functional email triage system today using Make.com and a low-cost AI model. This setup reliably classifies, routes, and prioritizes incoming messages without a dedicated support tool — expect to save 2-5 hours per week per team member using it.

Overview

Every ops team at a 1-50 person company deals with an overflowing inbox. The goal isn't to read every email — it's to ensure the right person sees the right message at the right time. An automated triage system does three things: classifies the email's intent, routes it to the appropriate person or team, and assigns a priority level (urgent, normal, low).

Make.com (formerly Integromat) is the glue. It watches your inbox, passes email content to an AI model (like OpenAI’s GPT-4 or Anthropic’s Claude), receives a structured response, and then performs actions based on that response. You don’t need to know how to code — just how to connect blocks.

This approach works best for generic inboxes like support@, info@, billing@, or hello@. It is not a replacement for a proper help desk tool like Zendesk or Freshdesk for teams over 15 people, but it is a solid bridge for smaller teams.

How the System Works (Step by Step)

  1. Trigger: Make.com watches a shared inbox (Gmail, Outlook, or IMAP). New email arrives.
  2. Extract: The automation pulls the subject line, sender email, and body text.
  3. Classify: The email content is sent to an AI model with a system prompt like: "Classify this email into one of these categories: sales inquiry, support ticket, billing issue, partnership request, or spam. Return only the category name and priority (urgent, normal, low)."
  4. Route: Based on the classification, the email is forwarded to specific team members, labels are applied, or a task is created in ClickUp.
  5. Log: A summary of the classification and routing is stored in Google Sheets or Airtable for review.

This entire flow runs in under 30 seconds per email. You can batch process old emails as well.

What You Need

  • Make.com account (free tier available, but the Pro plan at $9/month is likely needed for multiple scenarios)
  • An AI API key (OpenAI API costs $0.01–$0.03 per classification call; Claude is similar)
  • A shared email inbox (Gmail or Google Workspace works best)
  • (Optional) ClickUp or another task management tool for routing

Head-to-Head: AI Models for Classification

You have two solid options for the AI layer:

Option A: OpenAI GPT-4o-mini

  • Strength: Fast (sub-second responses), handles long email threads well, good at distinguishing spam from legitimate email
  • Weakness: Can hallucinate categories if the system prompt is not precise; slightly more expensive per call than Claude Haiku
  • Best for: Teams that handle a high volume of varied email types and need speed

Option B: Anthropic Claude Haiku

  • Strength: Cheaper per call, better at following strict output formats (JSON), excellent at avoiding "spam" false positives
  • Weakness: Slower than GPT-4o-mini (1-2 seconds per call), less reliable on very long email threads
  • Best for: Teams with a well-defined set of categories and predictable email types

Both work fine. If you are cost-sensitive, start with Claude Haiku. If you need raw speed, go with GPT-4o-mini.

Who Each Setup Is For

  • Make.com + GPT-4o-mini: For ops teams that get 50–200 emails per day from customers, partners, and random inquiries. You need speed and adaptability. Best for general triage.
  • Make.com + Claude Haiku: For teams that handle structured inquiries (e.g., only support tickets and billing). If your email volume is predictable, Claude is the cheaper, more consistent choice.
  • Make.com only (no AI): For teams under 5 people with very low volume (less than 20 emails/day). Use filters based on keywords or sender domains. Not recommended for scale.

Potential Pitfalls

  • Email parsing: HTML emails with heavy formatting sometimes cause the AI to misinterpret content. Use plain-text extraction if possible.
  • False positives: AI classification is not 100% accurate. Always include a "human review" step for anything classified as urgent. In Make, you can add a delay or a manual approval step.
  • Privacy: Emails containing PII or sensitive data going through an external API. Review your company's data policy before sending email content to OpenAI or Anthropic.
  • Spam handling: Basic spam filters (like Gmail's) should run before the Make scenario. Otherwise your AI will waste operations on spam
