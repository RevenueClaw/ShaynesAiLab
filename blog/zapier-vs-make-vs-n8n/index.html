Zapier vs Make vs n8n: When to Use Each Automation Tool

August 02, 2026

Quick Verdict

Use Zapier when your team needs to connect SaaS apps quickly with zero coding and you have budget for it. Use Make when you need visual, moderately complex workflows without breaking the bank. Use n8n when you want full control, self-hosting, or heavy data processing, and you have someone technical to set it up.

Overview

Zapier, Make (formerly Integromat), and n8n are the three most popular automation platforms for operations teams. They all let you connect apps and automate repetitive tasks, but they differ significantly in ease of use, pricing, logic capabilities, and hosting model.

Zapier is the simplest, most expensive option. Make offers a visual builder with more logic at a lower price. n8n is open-source, self-hostable, and the most powerful, but requires technical skills to deploy and maintain.

For a 1-50 person company, the right choice depends on your team's technical comfort, the complexity of your automations, and your budget.

Head-to-Head

Ease of Use

Zapier wins here. Its interface is straightforward: choose a trigger, choose an action, map fields. No coding required. Most non-technical team members can build basic Zaps in minutes.

Make has a visual drag-and-drop editor that's more powerful than Zapier's but has a steeper learning curve. The node-based layout can be intimidating at first, but once you're used to it, it's flexible.

n8n is the least beginner-friendly. You need to understand JSON, API concepts, and basic scripting to get the most out of it. The UI is functional but not polished.

Logic and Flexibility

Zapier supports basic conditional logic (filters, paths) but struggles with complex branching, loops, and data transformations. You can work around some limitations with Formatter, but it gets messy.

Make is significantly more flexible. You can build nested conditions, iterate over data arrays, perform aggregations, and use built-in functions for math and text manipulation. The visual editor makes complex logic easier to follow.

n8n is the most powerful. You can write custom JavaScript, use any REST API, and build truly complex workflows. It supports loops, error handling, webhooks, and node-based branching that rivals enterprise BPM tools.

Hosting and Control

Zapier and Make are both cloud-only. You don't control the infrastructure. This is fine for most teams, but you're subject to their uptime, rate limits, and privacy policies.

n8n can be self-hosted on your own server (Docker, Kubernetes, or local machine). This gives you full control over data, uptime, and scaling. For teams with compliance requirements or sensitive data, it's a major advantage.

Integrations

All three support hundreds of apps. Zapier has the largest library (5,000+ apps). Make and n8n have fewer pre-built integrations but cover the essentials (Google Suite, Slack, CRMs, email, databases). For anything custom, all three offer webhook and API-based connections.

Who Each Is For

Zapier is for:

  • Teams with no technical staff who need to connect SaaS apps quickly
  • Simple automations: form submissions → email, new lead → CRM, invoice → spreadsheet
  • Companies willing to pay for ease of use
  • Teams that don't need complex logic or data transformations

Make is for:

  • Operations teams with at least one person comfortable with visual tools
  • Moderately complex workflows: multi-step approval chains, data aggregation, conditional routing
  • Companies that want a balance of power and affordability
  • Teams that need to visualize and debug their automations visually

n8n is for:

  • Technical ops teams, developers, or startups with engineering support
  • Complex workflows with custom logic, APIs, or heavy data processing
  • Companies with data privacy or compliance requirements (self-hosting)
  • Teams that need unlimited workflows without per-task pricing

Final Thoughts

There's no single "best" automation tool. The right choice depends on your team's technical skill, the complexity of your workflows, and your budget. Start with Make if you want a strong middle ground. Use Zapier if your team is non-technical and you have the budget. Use n8n if you need control, power, or self-hosting.
