Small Team CRM Showdown: HubSpot vs Freshsales vs Pipedrive

September 17, 2026

Disclosure: Some links below are affiliate links. If you purchase through them, I may earn a commission at no extra cost to you. I only recommend tools I've tested or have direct experience with.

Quick Verdict

For most 1-50 person teams, Pipedrive wins on pure pipeline usability, Freshsales offers the best value for full-featured CRM needs, and HubSpot's free tier is only worth the hassle if you plan to upgrade to paid tiers later. If you need a simple, visual sales process and nothing else, pick Pipedrive. If you need email tracking, built-in phone, and automation without paying per-user add-ons, pick Freshsales. Avoid HubSpot free unless you're prepared for aggressive upselling and limited data visibility.

Overview

This comparison covers three CRMs commonly evaluated by ops leaders at small companies. I've tested all three with real sales workflows, imported messy CSV data, and connected them to common tools like Make.com and Fireflies.ai for call transcription. The focus here is practical: what works, what breaks, and what costs you time.

Head-to-Head

HubSpot Free CRM

What works: The free tier is genuinely free for up to 2 users with unlimited deals and contacts. The UI is clean, and the mobile app is solid. If you're already using HubSpot's marketing tools, the integration is seamless.

What breaks: The free tier limits you to one pipeline. You can't see revenue reports beyond basic dashboards. Email tracking is limited to 2,000 emails per user per month, which sounds fine until you hit it. The real problem is the data export — exporting your contacts and deals from the free tier is intentionally painful. You'll get CSV files with missing fields and no attachments.

Real-world pain: I've seen teams outgrow the free tier within 6 months. The jump from free to the lowest paid tier ($15/user/month) feels reasonable, but then you realize you need the $45/user/month tier to get custom objects or multiple pipelines. That's a 3x cost increase for basic functionality.

Freshsales

What works: Freshsales includes a built-in phone system (no extra cost for calls within the US/Canada), email tracking, and a decent mobile app. The free tier supports up to 3 users with unlimited deals and contacts, which is more generous than HubSpot's 2-user limit. The AI-powered lead scoring is actually useful — it's not a gimmick.

What breaks: The pipeline view is less visual than Pipedrive's. You get a list view with drag-and-drop, but it's not as fluid. The reporting is basic on lower tiers — you can't create custom reports unless you're on the Growth plan ($18/user/month). The automation rules are clunky; you'll likely end up using Make.com for complex workflows anyway.

Real-world pain: Freshsales' strength is also its weakness — it tries to do everything. You get a contact management system, a phone system, email, and a basic helpdesk. For a team of 5, this is overkill. For a team of 20, it's still not enough. You'll end up paying for features you don't use.

Pipedrive

What works: Pipedrive is the most visual pipeline tool I've tested. The drag-and-drop is smooth, and the interface is genuinely pleasant to use. The "Activities" feature — where you schedule follow-ups and calls directly on the deal — is a game-changer for sales reps who forget to log tasks. The mobile app is the best of the three.

What breaks: Pipedrive is not a full CRM — it's a pipeline management tool with contact storage. There's no built-in phone system (you'll need to integrate with something like Aircall or use Fireflies.ai for call recording). Email tracking is basic. The reporting is weak on the Essential plan ($14/user/month) — you only get basic funnels and revenue forecasts on the Advanced plan ($24/user/month).

Real-world pain: Pipedrive's simplicity is its limitation. If you need to track customer support tickets, marketing campaigns, or complex multi-touch attribution, this isn't the tool. You'll end up with a patchwork of integrations — Make.com for automation, Fireflies for call notes, and maybe ClickUp for project management. That works, but it's not a one-stop shop.

Who Each Is For

Choose HubSpot Free if: You're a solo founder or 2-person team, you're already using HubSpot's marketing tools, and you plan to upgrade to paid HubSpot within 6 months. Otherwise, skip it.

Choose Freshsales if: You need a full-featured CRM with built-in phone, email tracking, and lead scoring without paying extra. Best for teams of 3-15 that want one tool for sales and basic support.

Choose Pipedrive if: Your team lives and dies by the pipeline view. If your sales process is linear — lead → call → demo → proposal → close — and you want the least friction for reps to log activities, Pipedrive is the winner.
