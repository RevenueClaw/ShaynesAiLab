Small Team CRM Showdown: HubSpot vs Freshsales vs Pipedrive

July 30, 2026

Quick Verdict

If you need a free CRM that actually works for basic contact management and email tracking, start with HubSpot. If you need pipeline management that doesn’t require manual data entry, go with Pipedrive. Freshsales sits in the middle — better than HubSpot’s free tier for lead scoring, but not as clean for pure pipeline workflows.

Overview

For teams of 1 to 50 people, picking a CRM is less about feature count and more about what actually gets used. HubSpot’s free tier is the most popular entry point, but it has sharp limits. Freshsales offers AI-driven lead scoring at a reasonable price. Pipedrive is built around pipeline management and is the most visual of the three. All three integrate with Make.com for automation, and all three can sync with ClickUp for task management if you need that layer.

Head-to-Head: Pipeline Management

Pipeline management is where these CRMs differ most. Here’s how they handle the core workflow of moving deals from lead to close.

HubSpot Free

HubSpot’s free tier gives you a simple deal pipeline with drag-and-drop stages. You can track up to 1,000 contacts and log emails. The catch: you can only create one pipeline, and you’re limited to two custom deal stages. For a small team with a simple sales process, that might be fine. But if you need multiple pipelines (e.g., one for inbound leads, one for outbound), you’ll need to upgrade to the Starter plan at $15/month per user.

Freshsales

Freshsales has a more structured pipeline view. You get visual deal stages, and you can create multiple pipelines on the Growth plan ($18/user/month). The standout feature is Freddy AI, which scores leads based on engagement and fit. That’s useful if you’re drowning in leads but don’t want to manually qualify them. The downside: the UI feels busier than Pipedrive, and the learning curve is slightly steeper for ops leaders who just want to see deals moving.

Pipedrive

Pipedrive is the most pipeline-focused tool here. The entire interface is built around visual deal stages. You can drag deals between stages, set follow-up reminders, and create custom fields without hitting paywalls. On the Essential plan ($14/user/month), you get one pipeline. On Advanced ($34/user/month), you get multiple pipelines. For teams that live in their pipeline and need to see deal velocity at a glance, Pipedrive is the clearest option.

Who Each Is For

HubSpot Free

Best for teams that are just starting out and need a free way to store contacts, log emails, and track a single pipeline. If you’re a solo founder or a 2-person team testing out CRM usage, start here. The moment you need multiple pipelines or more than 1,000 contacts, you’ll outgrow it.

Freshsales

Best for teams that need lead scoring without manual effort. If you’re generating a lot of inbound leads (e.g., from content marketing or paid ads) and want the CRM to prioritize who to follow up with, Freshsales’ AI is genuinely useful. The Growth plan at $18/user/month is reasonable for teams of 5-20. The UI is more cluttered than Pipedrive, so it’s less ideal if you just want a clean pipeline view.

Pipedrive

Best for teams that live in their pipeline and need to track deal movement visually. If you’re in outbound sales, managing a multi-stage process, or need to see deal velocity at a glance, Pipedrive is the clearest choice. The Essential plan at $14/user/month is the cheapest paid option here. The trade-off: no built-in lead scoring, so you’ll need to manually qualify leads or use an external tool like Fireflies.ai to enrich call data.
