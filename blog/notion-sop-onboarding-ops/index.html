How Ops Teams Use Notion for SOPs and Onboarding

August 24, 2026

Disclosure: Some links below are affiliate links. If you purchase through them, I may earn a commission at no extra cost to you.

Quick Verdict

Notion is the best all-in-one tool for building and maintaining SOPs and onboarding workflows at companies under 50 people — provided your team is willing to invest a few hours in setup. It beats dedicated documentation tools on flexibility but loses on out-of-the-box structure.

Overview

Standard operating procedures and onboarding are two of the highest-leverage documents an ops team can produce. Done well, they turn tribal knowledge into repeatable processes. Done poorly, they become abandoned Google Docs that nobody updates.

Notion sits in a sweet spot for small ops teams. It's not a dedicated SOP tool (like Process Street or Trainual), and it's not a project management tool (like Asana or Monday.com). It's a flexible workspace that lets you build databases, wikis, and task lists in one place. For a 10-person company, that means your onboarding checklist, your IT setup guide, and your weekly ops review can all live in the same tool without jumping between tabs.

This article covers how to structure SOPs and onboarding in Notion, how it compares to a purpose-built alternative like ClickUp, and which tool makes sense for your team size and complexity.

Head-to-Head: Notion vs ClickUp for SOPs and Onboarding

Both Notion and ClickUp can handle SOPs and onboarding. But they approach it differently.

Notion

Strengths:

  • Database flexibility. You can create a "SOPs" database with properties for owner, last reviewed date, department, and status. Then build views: a table for the ops team, a gallery for new hires, a calendar for review dates.
  • Embedded content. Drop in a Loom video, a Google Sheet, or a Figma mockup directly into the page. New hires don't need to open six tabs.
  • Linking and backlinking. Reference a specific SOP inside an onboarding checklist, and the SOP page automatically shows which checklists link to it. This keeps your documentation interconnected without manual updates.
  • Templates. You can build one SOP template and reuse it. Same for onboarding sequences.

Weaknesses:

  • No native approval workflows. You can hack together a status property and assign a reviewer, but there's no "request approval → notify reviewer → lock page" flow without a third-party tool like Make.com.
  • No mandatory completion. You can't force a new hire to watch a video before marking a step done. It's trust-based.
  • Permissions are coarse. You can give edit access to a page or not. Granular role-based access (e.g., "can edit SOPs but not delete them") requires the Business plan.

ClickUp

Strengths:

  • Built-in task management. Onboarding steps can be actual tasks with assignees, due dates, dependencies, and checklists. You can see who is behind before they fall behind.
  • Approval flows. ClickUp Docs have a "request approval" feature. Someone reviews, approves or requests changes, and the doc locks. This matters for compliance-heavy SOPs.
  • Automations. "When status changes to Approved, notify the team in Slack" is a few clicks. No external tool needed.

Weaknesses:

  • Heavier setup. ClickUp has more features, which means more configuration. For a 5-person team, it can feel like using a forklift to move a chair.
  • Document experience is weaker. ClickUp Docs work fine, but they lack the rich block-based editing and database views that make Notion shine for reference material.
  • Pricing scales faster. The free plan is generous, but useful features like automations and granular permissions require paid tiers.

Who Each Is For

Notion is for ops teams that want one place for everything — SOPs, onboarding checklists, company wiki, meeting notes — and are comfortable spending a few hours setting up databases and templates. It works best when your team is already using Notion for other things. If you're starting from scratch, the learning curve is about two afternoons.

ClickUp is for ops teams that need to track onboarding as a project with deadlines, dependencies, and accountability. If your SOPs require formal approval before they go live, or if you need to report on who has completed which onboarding step, ClickUp's task-based structure saves you from building those workflows yourself.

A hybrid approach also works: use Notion for the SOP library and reference material, and use ClickUp for the onboarding project plan. Link from a ClickUp task to the relevant Notion page. This gives you the best of both without forcing one tool to do everything.

How to Build SOPs in Notion (Practical Steps)

If you decide to go with Notion, here's a structure that works for teams under 50 people:

  1. Create an SOPs database. Properties: Title, Owner (person), Department (select), Status (Draft / Published / Archived), Last Reviewed (date), Priority (Low / Medium / High).
  2. Build a template. Each SOP page should have: Purpose (why this exists), Steps (numbered or checklist), Tools Required (linked to a Tools database), Related SOPs (linked database entries).
  3. Create an Onboarding database. Properties: New Hire Name, Role, Start Date, Buddy (person), Status (Not Started / In Progress / Completed).
  4. Build an onboarding template. Each onboarding page should have: Week 1 checklist (linked SOPs), Week 2 checklist, tools to set up, people to meet, and a feedback section.
  5. Use linked databases. When you assign an SOP to a new hire, the SOP page automatically shows who has been assigned it. This gives you visibility into what new hires are reading without manual tracking.

For automation, you can use Make.com to trigger Slack notifications when an SOP status changes to "Needs Review" or when a new hire onboarding page is created. This keeps the workflow alive without logging into Notion every hour.

For recording SOP walkthroughs, Fireflies.ai can transcribe team meetings where you discuss process changes, and you can paste the summary into the SOP page. It's not essential, but it reduces the friction of documenting verbal decisions.
