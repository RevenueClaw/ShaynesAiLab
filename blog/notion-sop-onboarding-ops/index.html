How Ops Teams Use Notion for SOPs and Onboarding

September 22, 2026

Disclosure: Some links below are affiliate links. If you purchase through them, Shayne's AI Lab may earn a commission at no extra cost to you. We only recommend tools we have tested and believe are genuinely useful for ops teams.

Quick Verdict

Notion is a solid default for SOPs and onboarding at 1-50 person companies, but it is not a magic bullet. It works best when you treat it as a structured wiki with strict discipline, not as a freeform document dump. If you already live in Notion, use it. If you hate maintaining databases, consider a purpose-built tool like ClickUp or a simple Google Doc folder structure.

Overview

Operations leaders at growing companies face a common problem: knowledge lives in people's heads, Slack threads, and random Google Docs. Notion offers a centralized space to build SOPs and onboarding flows, but it requires deliberate structure. The core tension is flexibility versus discipline. Notion gives you infinite flexibility, which is great for small teams, but it becomes chaos without a clear taxonomy and ownership model.

For a 5-person team, Notion is overkill. For a 40-person team with multiple functions, it starts to make sense. The key is to design your workspace before you build it out. If you skip this step, you will end up with a digital junk drawer.

Head-to-Head: Notion vs. ClickUp vs. Dedicated SOP Tools

Let's compare Notion against the main alternatives ops teams actually consider.

Notion

Strengths:

  • All-in-one: SOPs, onboarding checklists, company wiki, and project trackers live in one place. No context switching.
  • Database power: You can create a "SOPs" database with properties like owner, status, and last reviewed date. Filter by department or priority.
  • Embed-friendly: Loom videos, Google Docs, and Figma files embed directly. This is huge for onboarding walkthroughs.
  • Pricing: Free for small teams (up to 10 guests), then $10/user/month for Business. Very cost-effective.

Weaknesses:

  • No built-in approval workflow: You cannot route an SOP for formal sign-off without a hack (e.g., status property + automations).
  • Version control is weak: There is no true version history for page content unless you manually duplicate pages or use the "Page History" feature (which is buried).
  • Offline mode is clunky: The desktop app works, but the mobile experience is slow. Not ideal for a warehouse or field team.
  • Search can be hit-or-miss: If your team does not use consistent tags, search returns irrelevant results.

ClickUp (for comparison)

ClickUp is a project management tool that also handles docs and SOPs. It is more structured out of the box.

Strengths: Native approval workflows, better task dependencies, and a more robust permission system. If your SOPs require sign-off from managers or compliance, ClickUp is stronger.

Weaknesses: Steeper learning curve. The UI is dense, and building a simple SOP can feel like configuring a database. For a non-technical ops hire, this can be frustrating.

Dedicated SOP Tools (e.g., Process Street, Trainual)

These are purpose-built for checklists and training. They offer features like conditional logic, task assignments, and detailed reporting. However, they cost more (typically $25-$50/user/month) and create a separate tool your team must remember to open.

Who Each Is For

Notion is for you if: You have a small team (under 25 people), you are comfortable with templates, and you want a single source of truth without paying for multiple tools. It is also ideal if you plan to embed videos and rich media in your SOPs.

ClickUp is for you if: You already use it for project management, or you need formal approval workflows. If your SOPs are linked to specific tasks (e.g., "Onboard new hire" has 15 sub-tasks), ClickUp integrates that seamlessly.

Dedicated tools are for you if: You have compliance requirements (ISO, SOC 2) or you need to track who completed which training. But be prepared for higher costs and less flexibility.

Practical Tips for Building SOPs in Notion

If you go with Notion, follow these rules to avoid chaos:

  • Create a single SOP database. Do not create separate pages for each SOP. Use one database with properties: Department, Owner, Status (Draft/Review/Live), Last Reviewed.
  • Use a template button. Create a "New SOP" template that auto-fills with sections: Purpose, Scope, Definitions, Step-by-Step, Troubleshooting, and Version History.
  • Assign an owner for every SOP. If no one owns it, it will go stale within 6 months.
  • Embed Loom videos for walkthroughs. Text-only SOPs are rarely followed. Record a 2-minute video showing the process, then paste the Loom link into the SOP.
  • Set a quarterly review reminder. Use Notion's reminder feature on the database or a recurring task in your project tracker.

Onboarding Workflows in Notion

For onboarding, create a "New Hire" database with a template that includes:

  • Day 1 checklist (accounts, laptop, intro meetings)
  • 30-60-90 day goals
  • Link to role-specific SOPs
  • Team contact list

One tip: Use a separate database for onboarding tasks and link it to the employee's profile. This way, you can track progress without digging through Slack history.

Final Thoughts

Notion is not perfect, but it is the most pragmatic choice for most ops teams at this stage. It is cheap, flexible, and your team likely already knows how to use it. The failure mode is not the tool—it is the lack of structure. Spend an afternoon setting up your database and templates, assign owners, and commit to a review cadence. Do that, and Notion will handle your SOPs and onboarding just fine.

If you need automation to connect Notion with other tools (e.g., send a Slack message when an SOP is updated), consider using Make.com to build custom workflows without code. For meeting notes and action items during onboarding, Fireflies.ai can transcribe and summarize automatically, which you can then paste into Notion.
