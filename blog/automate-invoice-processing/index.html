How to Automate Invoice Processing with Jotform and Make

July 31, 2026

Quick Verdict

Jotform + Make.com is a solid, low-cost way to automate invoice intake and routing for small teams. It won't replace an ERP, but it eliminates manual email forwarding and spreadsheet hunting for most service businesses under 50 people.

Overview

Invoice processing is a pain point for ops teams at small companies. You get PDFs in email, Slack attachments, maybe a shared Dropbox folder. Someone has to open each one, check the amount, confirm the vendor, and decide who needs to approve it. Then you track payment status manually.

Jotform handles the intake side — a simple form where vendors or internal staff upload invoices. Make.com (formerly Integromat) handles the routing and notifications. Together, they replace the manual triage step. The workflow looks like this:

  • Vendor submits invoice via Jotform (upload PDF, enter amount, vendor name, PO number if applicable)
  • Jotform stores the submission and triggers a Make scenario
  • Make checks the amount against a threshold (e.g., under $500 goes to team lead, over $500 goes to finance manager)
  • Make sends a notification to the right person via email or Slack with a link to the submission
  • Approver clicks a link to view the invoice and mark it approved or rejected (via a simple Jotform approval form or a status update)
  • Make updates a Google Sheet or your accounting tool with the final status

This isn't a full AP automation solution — it won't extract line items or match POs automatically. But for teams processing 20-50 invoices a month, it cuts the manual routing time by about 70%.

Jotform isn't the only form builder, but it's the best fit here. Let's compare it to the obvious alternatives:

Who Each Is For

Jotform is for:

  • Teams that want a simple, customizable intake form without coding
  • Operations leaders who need built-in approval workflows (Jotform Approvals)
  • Companies with non-technical staff who need to submit invoices (vendors, contractors)
  • Anyone who wants a visual form builder with conditional logic that actually works

Jotform is NOT for:

  • Teams processing 500+ invoices per month (you need a proper AP tool like Bill.com or QuickBooks Online)
  • Companies that need OCR to extract line-item data from PDFs automatically
  • Teams that want a single platform for full AP lifecycle (Jotform is intake + routing only)

Make.com is for:

  • Teams that need to connect Jotform to other tools (Slack, email, Google Sheets, accounting software)
  • Operations leaders comfortable with visual workflow builders (drag-and-drop, no code)
  • Companies that want conditional routing based on invoice amount, vendor, or department

Make.com is NOT for:

  • Teams that want a pre-built invoice processing template (you'll build it yourself)
  • Users who want a single tool for everything (Make is middleware, not an end solution)
  • Teams that need real-time sync with QuickBooks or Xero (it works, but can be finicky)

Building the Workflow (Step-by-Step)

Here's the practical setup, assuming you have a Jotform account and a Make.com account:

  1. Create your Jotform invoice intake form. Add fields: Vendor Name, Invoice Number, Amount, Invoice Date, Upload PDF (file upload widget). Add a dropdown for "Department" if you route by team. Set up conditional logic: if Amount > $500, show an additional "Requires CFO Approval" checkbox.
  2. Enable Jotform Approvals. Go to Settings > Approvals. Create a simple approval flow: one approver for invoices under $500, a different approver for over $500. Jotform will email the approver a link to approve/reject.
  3. Create a Make scenario. Add a Jotform module (trigger: "New Submission"). Map the fields from the form into Make variables.
  4. Add routing logic. Use a Router module in Make. Create two routes: one for invoices under $500 (send Slack message to team lead), one for over $500 (send email to finance manager with approval link).
  5. Add a Google Sheets module. After routing, add a row to a spreadsheet with: timestamp, vendor, amount, status (Pending), approval link. This becomes your invoice log.
  6. Add a webhook for approval updates. When the approver marks the invoice as approved in Jotform, trigger a second Make scenario that updates the Google Sheet row status to "Approved" and sends a confirmation email to the vendor.

Total setup time: 2-3 hours for someone familiar with both tools. The most time-consuming part is testing the approval callback.

Limitations to Know

  • Jotform's approval workflow is basic. You can't have multi-step approvals (e.g., manager then CFO) without building custom logic in Make.
  • File uploads from Jotform are stored on Jotform's servers. If you need them in your own cloud storage, add a Make module to copy the file to Google Drive or Dropbox.
  • Make.com's free tier has a 1,000 operations/month limit. Each invoice submission might use 3-5 operations (trigger, router, sheet update, notification). At 50 invoices/month, that's 150-250 operations — fine. At 200 invoices, you'll need the Pro plan.
  • No native integration with accounting software like QuickBooks or Xero. You'd need to add a third step (e.g., Zapier or a custom API call) to push approved invoices into your accounting tool.
