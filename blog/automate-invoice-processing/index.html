How to Automate Invoice Processing with Jotform and Make

July 31, 2026

Quick Verdict

Jotform + Make.com is a solid, low-cost way to automate invoice intake and routing for small teams. It’s not a full accounting system, but it eliminates manual email-to-pdf forwarding and spreadsheet tracking. Expect to spend 2-4 hours setting it up, and you’ll save roughly 5-10 hours per month on invoice processing.

Overview

If you’re running operations for a 1-50 person company, invoice processing probably looks like this: vendors email PDFs, someone downloads them, renames them, saves them to a folder, and manually enters data into accounting software or a spreadsheet. It’s repetitive, error-prone, and scales poorly.

You can fix most of this with two tools: Jotform for intake and Make.com for routing and notifications. Neither is a full ERP. But together, they handle the grunt work so your team only touches invoices when something needs a decision.

Here’s the workflow we’ll build:

  1. Vendor submits invoice via a Jotform form (with file upload).
  2. Make.com picks up the submission, extracts the PDF, and renames it.
  3. Make routes the file to a shared folder (Google Drive, Dropbox) and sends a notification to your ops channel (Slack, email).
  4. Optionally, Make logs the invoice data to a Google Sheet or ClickUp task for approval tracking.

No coding required. You’ll need a Jotform account (free or paid) and a Make.com account (free tier works for low volume).

Head-to-Head: Jotform vs. Alternatives for Invoice Intake

Jotform isn’t the only form builder. Here’s how it compares to the main alternatives for this specific use case.

Feature Jotform Google Forms Typeform
File upload (PDF) Yes, up to 1GB per file (paid plans) Yes, but limited to 10GB total per form Yes, but only on paid plans
Conditional logic Advanced (field visibility, calculations) Basic (section routing only) Good (branching logic)
Integration with Make.com Native, reliable Via Google Sheets trigger (indirect) Native, but fewer triggers
Custom branding Yes, on paid plans Limited (must use Google theme) Yes, on paid plans
Price for small teams Free (100 submissions/month) or $39/month (Bronze) Free Free (10 responses/month) or $25/month (Essentials)

Why Jotform wins here: It has the best balance of file upload limits, conditional logic, and Make.com integration. Google Forms is free but clunky for file-heavy workflows. Typeform looks nicer but limits file uploads on free tier and has fewer Make triggers.

Weakness: Jotform’s free plan caps submissions at 100/month and only stores 10MB of uploaded files. For a growing team, you’ll likely need the Bronze plan ($39/month) or higher.

Who Each Tool Is For

Jotform

Best for: Teams that need a customizable, branded intake form for vendors. If you want to collect specific fields (PO number, department, amount) alongside the PDF upload, Jotform’s conditional logic makes it easy to show/hide fields based on vendor type or invoice amount.

Not for: Teams that need OCR or automatic data extraction from PDFs. Jotform doesn’t read invoice data from uploaded files. You’ll need to ask vendors to fill in fields manually, or pair Jotform with a tool like DocParser or Rossum (which adds cost).

Make.com

Best for: Ops teams that want to connect Jotform to multiple destinations without writing code. Make’s visual builder is intuitive for non-technical users, and its error handling (retries, fallbacks) is better than most low-code alternatives.

Not for: Teams that need real-time sync or high-volume processing (thousands of invoices per month). Make’s free tier has a 1,000 operation/month limit, and paid plans can get expensive if you’re running complex multi-step scenarios. Also, Make’s debugging can be frustrating — you’ll want to test each step carefully.
